# BDB Games

Two games starring BDB. Made for laughs, all roasts are fictional.

Play: https://terryjones1995.github.io/BDB/

## Charleston Solitaire (`/solitaire`)
Klondike with BDB on all 52 cards. Draw 1 or 3, drag or tap to move, undo, hints, auto-finish,
best time, and a roast every time you deal a card (525 lines in the deck). Win and BDB shows up as a paper cutout to celebrate.


## Purrjong (`/mahjong`)

Mahjong solitaire with cats instead of BDB, on a 3D table: one self-contained page (`mahjong/index.html`) that borrows three.js from `chess/vendor`.
- The tiles are real blocks (ivory on top, amber underneath) on a felt mat, lit and shadowed, and they move: they drop in on the deal, lift when picked, fly together and clack when matched, and flip over on a shuffle
- Every face is drawn in SVG in the page and engraved into the tile. Paws, fish and yarn run 1 to 9, there are seven cats, and any toy matches any toy and any treat any treat
- Five wide boards for a screen on its side (Yarn Ball 52, Paw Print 82, Cat Face 88, Goldfish 108, Turtle 144) and five tall ones for a phone held upright (the same, with Cat Tower 144 in place of Turtle), so the tiles stay big either way
- Each deal is built by taking the board apart two free tiles at a time and filling those spots with a matching pair, so it can always be cleared; Shuffle does the same with the tiles that are left
- Hint, undo, a timer and a best time per board, saved on the device
- The picture steps its own quality down if frames come slowly, and a device that cannot draw 3D at all gets flat tiles instead (`?2d=1` forces them; `?q=0` to `?q=3` pins the quality; `?slow=20` plays every movement in slow motion)

## Charleston Chess (`/chess`)
3D chess against BDB on a parquet court.
- Play BDB: five levels, from Rec League up to Pro (full-strength Stockfish)
- Play friends online: lobby of everyone on the chess page, challenges, invite links, clocks,
  chat and taunts, draw offers, rematches. Peer-to-peer (no server), so a few strict networks may block it
- 500+ trash-talk lines shared across both games (`assets/roasts.js`)
- Every piece has its own kill move: Pawn Shiv, Horse Kick, Diagonal Slice, Tower Slam, Royal Blast, King's Stomp
- BDB trash talks every capture and calls out forced mates ("Mate in 4. Trash can.")
- Undo, hints, promotion picker, and the full rules (castling, en passant, draws)

## Turbo Kart Rally (`/kart`)
Arcade kart racing against seven AI drivers on Palm Cove Circuit, three.js, keyboard or controller.
It's BridgeMind's game (MIT, https://github.com/bridge-mind/turbo-kart-rally), copied unchanged from
Patch's port `blendi/turbo-kart-rally` (commit 24da027). Our remix on Patch is `terry/turbo-kart-rally`.
Its LICENSE and CREDITS.md are in `kart/`.
- Race: you against seven AI drivers, as in the original.
- Online: up to 8 friends in a room (create a room, share the code or the `kart/?room=CODE` link), with AI
  filling empty grid slots. It runs on the BDB Supabase server's realtime channels (`api.bigdickb.ca`,
  public anon key in `kart/src/net-config.js`); no tables. Each player's browser drives its own kart and the
  host's drives the AI; the others see them as interpolated "ghost" karts (`kart/src/online.js`, `lobby.js`).
- The server's realtime limits are raised to 1000 events/s by `~/bin/bdb-realtime-limits.sh` (cron, every
  10 min), since the realtime container resets them to 100 on every restart.
- `connect4/` Charleston Four: four in a row vs BDB, pass-and-play, or online
- `blocks/` Break Room Blocks: falling-blocks puzzle
- `tycoon/` BDB Tycoon: property-trading board game for 2–6 (bots, pass-and-play, online tables; host-authoritative, bots cover dropped seats)
- `city/` BDB City (working title): a 3D city builder in three.js r185. Draw roads, zone land and a city grows along them, with traffic, weather, day and night. An early build: no saving yet, and it wants a computer rather than a phone. Built on Fable Cities by raw (MIT, CC0 art; what was changed is in `city/NOTICE.md`), with no build step. What comes next (saving, crime and fire crews, an airport, deals between cities, friends) is in `city/PLAN.md`, and the models being made for it in `city/ASSETS.md`
- `craft/` BDB Craft: a creative mode block game for computers, in three.js with no build step. Endless worlds made from a seed (eleven biomes, caves, ores, four kinds of tree, or flat), 353 blocks with their own pixel art painted by code (`tex.js`: every wood, the stones, all 16 colours of wool, concrete, powder, terracotta, glazed terracotta and stained glass, ores and deepslate ores, flowers, lights, lava; shaped blocks: slabs (two make a block), stairs, fences, gates, walls, panes, iron bars, doors, trapdoors, carpets, torches, lanterns, ladders, chains, each with a run of ids for the way it faces and for standing in water so things can be built under water (`shapes.js` has their boxes for drawing, bumping into and picking); logs lie on their side and furnaces face you; a Controls screen changes any key or mouse button, Shift sneaks; redstone (`redstone.js`: dust that carries power 15 blocks, levers, buttons, pressure plates, redstone torches that invert, repeaters, lamps, doors, TNT and powered rails, run by the host ten times a second), rails that lay themselves round corners and up slopes, minecarts you ride (`carts.js`), TNT that blows round holes; water and lava that flow from their source (water 7 blocks, lava 3, falling straight down open drops, drying up when the source goes, making obsidian or cobblestone where they meet; `liquids.js`, run by the host), buckets, sand and gravel that fall, concrete powder that sets in water; worlds made after the world update use `gen2.js` (jungle, bamboo, savanna, desert, badlands, swamp, taiga, snowy taiga, cherry groves, meadows, mushroom islands, peaks, rivers, warm oceans with coral, icebergs, deepslate, every ore, lava pools, springs that make waterfalls, mineshafts with rails, villages, desert wells, igloos, shipwrecks, ruined portals) while older worlds keep `gen.js`; ids are 16 bits and only ever added to, and worlds saved with the old 8-bit chunks are widened when they load), walking, sprinting and flying, break, place and pick, a hotbar and an inventory of every block. Chunks are 16 x 16 x 128, made and meshed in web workers (`gen.js`, `mesh.js`: greedy meshing, corner shadows, sky light that spreads under roofs and into caves, and warm light from torches, lamps, glowstone and lava that reaches 8 blocks), drawn from one texture array. Worlds save to the player's account (the Break Room Brawl one) through `craft/supabase.sql`, which has to be run once in Supabase after `brawl/supabase.sql`, or to the browser without it. Players sign in with their BDB account the first time and make a blocky character (skin, hair, eyes, face, extras, top, colours, jersey number; `skin.js`), kept on the account; F5 shows yourself, and in first person you see your own arm holding the block. Days last 20 minutes with a sun, a moon that changes phase, stars, sunsets and 3D clouds (`sky.js`; the host keeps the time, `/time day` or `night` in chat sets it, and it can be switched to always day). Animals (cows, pigs, sheep, chickens, horses, rabbits, wolves, foxes, cats, goats, llamas, by biome), villagers in villages and, at night, zombies, husks, skeletons that shoot, creepers that blow up, spiders and endermen (`mobs.js`; the host runs them). Everything has health and a bar over its head when hurt; swords, axes, a bow and a trident do real damage; spawn eggs for every one; with "Monsters can hurt you" on you have ten hearts, take falls and lava, and start again at the spawn when they run out. Water and lava move on screen. Weather (`weather.js`): clear spells, rain and thunderstorms come and go (the host decides); snow falls in cold biomes and high up, rain elsewhere, nothing in deserts, badlands and savannas, and none under a roof; storms darken the sky and throw lightning, and in open country a storm can spin up a tornado that wanders, throws you and the animals about and tears up plants and leaves. `/weather clear`, `rain`, `thunder` or `tornado` in chat. Friends join a world with a six letter code, or from Friends building on the start screen, over the chess transport: the host's copy is the real one, only chunks someone changed travel, and only the host saves (`party.js`) Cities and towns in new worlds (three sizes, up to huge cities whose towers reach the height limit, named after real places), with streets, parks, cars and street lamps, the city's name in giant letters in the sky and a welcome title as you walk in; Belleville and Kingston, Ontario, are built after the real places (Belleville City Hall's clock tower, the Moira River and the Bay Bridge; Kingston City Hall, Confederation Park, Market Square, Fort Henry, the LaSalle Causeway, Murney Tower and Grant Hall). Teleport to a city from the pause menu (searchable, nearest first), or `/city`, `/city <name>` and `/tp x y z`.
- `fleet/` Sink BDB: hide your fleet and hunt his, vs BDB at 3 levels or online 1v1 with a fair-play check
- `brawl/` Break Room Brawl: 3D lane battles in three.js. 248 characters, buildings, spells and traps in 10 builds each (2,480 cards). Every card has one fixed rarity and one of 12 types, and stacking a type in a deck makes it fight harder. Above Legendary sits a God tier (Thor, Death and Zeus so far: powers that reach the whole map, one God to a deck, found in the Divine Chest). Mana comes in half as fast again every two minutes of a match. A deck holds at most 7 Rare, 5 Epic, 3 Legendary and 1 God. Card levels add 3% each and change the card's colour every two levels. A daily reward, three jobs a day (an easy one, a middling one and a hard one, with a chest for doing all three), a win streak and a tower bounty sit on the hub, eight career milestones on the Profile pay a chest for every rung (and upgrade tokens for the higher ones), and BDB brings his cards up to within two levels of a deck that has outgrown its place on the ladder. Abilities live in the rules (`sim.js`): landing blasts, charges, stealth, chain lightning, summons, Mana farms, tower shields. A 1,000-level ladder in ten tiers, quick battles at five skill levels (Easy to Legend), raised odds that pay more (one against two or three BDBs), a win that pays more for every tower taken, two minutes of overtime at four times the Mana, chests with stated odds, 43 ready-made decks, player upgrades bought with tokens from the ladder, a deck builder for new players (the starter deck is bought from a budget of 1,500 coins, and 150,000 more land once it is built), three deck slots that can sit part-built, cards that can be sold back to the Market for a price set by rarity alone (20, 80, 300, 1,200 and 8,000 coins from Common to God; one from its sheet or several at once from the Deck tab, never a card that is in a deck and never the last deck's worth), four graphics settings that step down by themselves on a slow device, 15 arenas (five built in 3D, ten painted floors in `brawl/maps/` that each ladder tier calls home, with the river and bridges read off the painting), device saves with a save code (`store.js` brings old saves up to date), online 1v1 and co-op for two against two or three BDBs at a chosen level, with emotes, pause and a rematch from the result screen (host-authoritative over the chess transport), and a running head-to-head score against every real opponent. Models: KayKit character packs by Kay Lousberg and Ultimate Monsters by Quaternius (all CC0, see `brawl/models/SOURCES.txt`), sharing clip libraries (`am.glb`, `al.glb`) and a weapon rack (`wp.glb`); the forty-odd hero characters (the rigs marked `R` in `brawl/rigs.js`) were generated, rigged and animated with Hyper3D Rodin for this game; the hundred-odd `T_` characters were generated with Tripo and rigged for this game by a fitter of its own (a 20-joint skeleton found from the shape of each T-pose model, or taken from the model's own skeleton when it came with one, and skin weights by bone heat through a voxel copy of it), and all of them play one shared clip library, `aG.glb`. Two are different: the horse (`BlackHorse.glb`) is a Tripo model rigged and animated with Rodin, and the Headless Horseman (`T_Horseman.glb`) is one solid model that is rocked by code. Model files are meshopt-compressed and loaded per match; card pictures in `brawl/art/` are rendered from the models. Scenery in `brawl/props/` is bundled from KayKit Medieval Hexagon, City Builder, Furniture, Restaurant and Dungeon packs (all CC0). Pan/zoom camera, tap-to-inspect, bloom on desktop. Nothing just vanishes when it dies: the rules say what finished each troop and where the blow came from (a `dead` event), and the view picks how the body takes it (thrown clear by a blast or a big hit, toppled, frozen where it stands, dropped out of the sky, flattened by a trap) and what is left of it (burnt to ash, shattered ice, a puddle, lightning that lights it up from the inside, and by type: bones, scrap and sparks, falling leaves, flying paperwork, a column of light, a shadow that swallows it, a soul that leaves). Loose pieces come from two instanced pools, and with reduced motion on everything simply fades. Troops take orders: tap one of yours (or a building that shoots), then tap an enemy troop, building or tower, and it goes for that, past anything nearer, until one of them is gone (`order` in `sim.js`; a guest's order goes to the host the way a card does, a taunt still cannot be ignored, and tapping the troop again calls it off). A troop that finishes three enemies in one life goes on a rampage and gets a perk set by its type (`RAMPAGE` in `cards.js`: Dragon Fury, Wildfire, Cold Snap, Mana Surge, Soul Feast, Smoke, On the Scent, Overgrowth, War Frenzy, Rally, Overclock, Coffee Break), and steps up again at six. The perks ride on a small buff engine in the rules (a list on the unit, totals the rules read) that Boost cards will share. Every card has four goals that each pay coins once, 992 in all (`goalsOf` in `cards.js`: times played, wins with it in the deck, enemies it finished, towers it finished, with stand-ins for cards that cannot do those). The rules keep count per card, including whatever a card summons and whatever its fire or poison finishes; the counts reach a guest in the `over` event, and progress lives in the save. Boosts are a second, small deck that costs no Mana: 504 of them (`BOOSTS` in `cards.js`: fourteen effects, from Lightning Speed and Iron Skin to Second Chance, each for every one of the twelve types, in three strengths). A Boost is dropped on a spot and reaches every troop of its side standing there, whatever its type; troops of the Boost's own type get half as much again. A player keeps a Boost deck of eight for each deck slot, is dealt one every 30 seconds of a match and holds six at most (with a full hand nothing more arrives). Everyone is given twelve plain ones for the types of their deck; the Market sells a Boost Box and every Boost singly. BDB brings his own from the eleventh rung of the ladder up, and uses them: the ones that mend on troops that are hurt, the ones that protect or hit harder on troops in a fight. A guest's Boost deck travels with its hello, and its plays go to the host the way cards do. A player's first match waits for their first card and shows four tips as they come to matter. While a match loads, both sides are shown on name plates with a tip under them, and the result screen names the troop that did the most damage. The game can be added to a phone's home screen (`brawl/manifest.webmanifest`), where it opens without the browser around it. A service worker (`brawl/sw.js`) keeps a copy of the game on the device: the page is asked for fresh each time, files with a version in their address come straight from the copy, and models and pictures are shown from the copy and refreshed behind the scenes, so the game opens at once and plays against BDB with no signal (open it once with `?nosw=1` to throw the copy away). Sound is in `brawl/audio.js`: one file of effects and one looping battle theme in `brawl/snd/` (where each came from is in `brawl/snd/CREDITS.md`), balanced against each other by measured loudness; in the menus the music is muffled, as if the match were next door. Coins, crowns, chests, the trophy and the Mana crystal in `brawl/ui/` are pictures of Tripo models. Accounts, cloud saves, the online list, invites, trading and the league table (everyone with an account, by ladder level, then trophies) go through the SQL functions in `brawl/supabase.sql` (run it once in the Supabase SQL editor; the project URL and anon key are in `brawl/config.js`). Without it the game falls back to saves on the device. Battle Royale puts you and four BDBs in the Octagon, each with a King Tower and two guard towers, no teams and Mana x2 from the start: lose your King Tower and you are out, last one standing wins (`maps/ring.glb` is the arena: made in Tripo, then levelled, sized so its inner wall is the edge of play, and thinned to 40k triangles). Tapping a player in Who's on or the league table opens their profile (level, record, milestones, decks), and from there a trade offer: up to five cards and some coins each way, answered whenever the other player is next on (accept, counter or turn down). What an offer gives is held on the server until it is answered (`brawl_offers` and `brawl_mail` in `supabase.sql`); deliveries reach the save once, by id.

New games start from `assets/TEMPLATE.html`, which uses the shared `assets/kit.css` and `assets/kit.js`.
